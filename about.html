About Hikaru Foundation
“Bangladesh's digital future should belong to its people.”
Our vision

A digitally empowered world where technology and innovation ensure justice, equity, and social welfare.

Our mission

To apply emerging technologies to real-world challenges, promoting social justice and inclusive development. We work to improve digital literacy, promote ethical technological innovation, and advance technology policy through collaborative research and engagement.

The origin story

Bangladesh's digital landscape runs on infrastructure it does not own.

Foreign companies hold exclusive control and centralized data centers hold a monopoly. International business organizations collect and use citizen data for their own commercial interests, an uncontrolled interference in the data rights and freedoms of people here.

That was the observation the work started from in 2021. Not a product idea, a dependency: every layer of the country's digital life sitting on infrastructure owned and governed somewhere else, and no domestic alternative being built.

A sovereign blockchain decentralizes that control. It makes activity transparent, secures access to financial and civic systems, and returns the digital commons to the people who depend on it. Everything Hikaru Foundation does follows from that one conclusion.

2021

The work begins

Research and groundwork start, driven by a single question: what would it take for Bangladesh to run digital infrastructure it actually controls?

March 2024

Onchain Software and Research Limited established

The research effort takes formal shape as a registered entity, giving the work a legal home and a structure to build from.

2025

Hikaru Layer-1 project initiated

Development starts on the sovereign Layer-1 network: an immutable ledger run on community infrastructure, built to carry payments, supply chain records, and digital identity.

2026

Hikaru Foundation

The foundation brings the three initiatives under one purpose: the network, the research and policy work, and Hikaru Academy.

What we hold to

Five commitments, not slogans

These are the standards the work is judged against, including by us. Each one is testable: you can look at what we ship and tell whether we kept it.

Transparency and accountability

A ledger anyone can inspect is only useful if the organization behind it works the same way. We publish what we decide and why, and we expect to be held to it.

Ethical innovation

New technology is not neutral, and speed is not a defence. We weigh who carries the risk of what we build before we build it.

Decentralized governance

Infrastructure the public depends on should not have a single owner, and that includes us. Control is designed to distribute as the network grows.

Sustainability

Infrastructure that outlives its funding cycle is the only kind worth building. We plan for decades of operation, not a launch.

Technological autonomy

A country that cannot run its own critical systems is negotiating from a weak position permanently. Autonomy is the whole point of the work.

Knowledge in public hands

Infrastructure people cannot understand is not really theirs. Hikaru Academy exists so the knowledge spreads as widely as the network does.
