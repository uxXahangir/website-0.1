Initiative 01 In development

A ledger the country runs itself.

Hikaru is a sovereign Layer-1 blockchain powered by community-driven decentralized infrastructure. It maintains an immutable ledger, enables secure peer-to-peer interaction, and carries payments, supply chain records, and digital identity.

What it is

The short version

What problem it solves

Almost every digital service you use in Bangladesh keeps its records on computers owned by a company somewhere else. Your payment history, your identity documents, the record that a shipment arrived. Someone else holds the original, and you hold a copy they let you see.

That arrangement works right up until it doesn't. The owner changes their terms, raises their price, gets acquired, is compelled by a foreign government, or simply switches you off.

Hikaru is an attempt to keep the original here, held by many independent people at once rather than one company.

What problem it solves

Critical national record-keeping currently depends on custodial infrastructure operated under foreign jurisdiction. That creates a single point of both control and failure, with no domestic recourse when terms, pricing, or legal compulsion change upstream.

A Layer-1 with domestically distributed validation moves the authoritative record inside the jurisdiction and removes the single custodian, at the cost of the usual consistency and throughput trade-offs.

The design target is sovereignty and censorship resistance first, throughput second. L1 rather than a rollup because settlement assurance cannot be inherited from a chain outside the jurisdiction.

How it actually works

Instead of one company keeping the record, hundreds of independent computers each keep an identical copy. When something new happens, they all check it and all write it down together.

Changing a past entry would mean convincing most of those machines simultaneously, which is hard on purpose. That difficulty is the security.

Anyone can run one of those machines. That is what makes it belong to the public rather than to us.

How it actually works

Independent validators maintain replicated state and reach agreement on ordering through consensus. Blocks commit once a supermajority signs, and history is protected by the cost of rewriting it.

Rewriting committed history requires controlling a supermajority of stake or validators, so the security budget is a function of how widely and independently validation is distributed, not of any cryptographic claim on its own.

Validator entry is permissionless by design. Concentration of validation is the primary threat to the sovereignty property this network exists to provide.

What it carries

Four applications

None of these are live. Each describes what the network is designed to support.

Application 01

Financial transactions

Sending value directly to another person, with the record of it kept by the network rather than by a company that can freeze it, price it, or lose it.

Peer-to-peer value transfer with settlement finality on the native ledger. No custodial intermediary in the settlement path and no dependency on correspondent infrastructure outside the jurisdiction.

  • No single company can block a payment between two people
  • The record survives even if any one operator disappears
  • Works the same whether the amount is large or tiny
  • Censorship resistance scales with validator decentralization
  • Finality guarantees depend on the consensus parameters, still being set
  • Fee model must not price out low-value domestic transfers
Application 02

Supply chain tracking

A record of where something came from and who handled it, written as it happens and impossible to quietly edit afterwards.

Append-only custody attestations anchored on-chain. The ledger guarantees the record was not altered after commitment; it cannot guarantee the record was true when written.

  • Provenance claims can be checked, not just trusted
  • Exporters can prove chain of custody to buyers abroad
  • Nobody can rewrite the history after a problem appears
  • The oracle problem is unsolved here and always will be
  • Integrity of input data remains an off-chain governance question
  • Useful mainly where the dispute is about tampering, not accuracy
Application 03

Digital identity management

Credentials you hold and present yourself, instead of records held about you by organisations you never chose and cannot audit.

Holder-controlled credentials with selective disclosure. Personal data stays off-chain by design; only proofs and revocation state touch the ledger.

  • You show only the fact needed, not your whole file
  • No central database of citizens to breach or sell
  • Works offline where the network reaches unevenly
  • Personal data on-chain would be irreversible and unlawful. Never do it.
  • Revocation and key recovery are the hard problems, not issuance
  • Highest-risk application on this list. Slowest to ship, correctly.
Application 04

Community node infrastructure

The network runs on machines contributed by the people who use it. This is not a feature of the system. It is the system.

Permissionless validation with hardware requirements set deliberately low. Decentralization is the security property; every efficiency gain that concentrates validation erodes the thing being built.

  • Anyone can run one, which is what keeps it public
  • No single operator can be pressured into shutting it down
  • Ownership is distributed by participation, not by decree
  • Minimum spec must stay reachable on hardware available locally
  • Geographic and operator diversity matter more than node count
  • Concentration is the failure mode to monitor from day one

Where it stands

No dates we can't keep

Phases, not deadlines. An infrastructure project that misses published launch dates loses more credibility than one that declines to publish them.

Complete

Groundwork

Research from 2021, formalised as Onchain Software and Research Limited in March 2024.

Current

Protocol design

Layer-1 project initiated 2025. Consensus, security model, and node requirements under active work.

Next

Public testnet

Open validation, community operators, and the first real-world failure reports.

Later

Mainnet

Only once decentralization, security review, and the regulatory picture all support it.
